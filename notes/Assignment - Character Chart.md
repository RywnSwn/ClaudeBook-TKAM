## Official format
One row per character. Columns exactly as given:
1. **Character Traits** - what kind of person are they
2. **Evidence from Novel** - quotations or moments, with page #
3. **Characterization strategy** - Narration, Dialogue, Actions, or Others' Views
4. **Author's Purpose** - why this trait matters to the story or themes

Rows, in the order the teacher listed them: Atticus Finch, Scout Finch,
Jem Finch, Dill, Boo Radley, Tom Robinson, Mayella Ewell, Bob Ewell.

Page numbers: our source text files don't have page numbers in them, so
that part of each Evidence cell is left blank below. Fill those in from the
physical copy/edition being used in class if the assignment needs them.

## How this maps onto our STEAL workflow
Columns 1-3 line up almost directly with what we already log per chapter:
- Character Traits = our "what it reveals" line
- Evidence = our quote
- Characterization Strategy = our technique label ("Others' Views" =
  our Effect on others STEAL category, just named differently)

Column 4, Author's Purpose, is the new judgment call: does a moment connect
to a bigger story/theme, or is it more just character color/texture? Not
every trait needs to be inflated into a big thematic claim, "mostly color"
is a legitimate answer too.

## Additional STEAL moments on file (not yet promoted into the chart above)
These are extra traits/evidence pulled from the Ch1-6 trackers, in case a
different trait ends up being a better fit for a character's single chart
row later, or the assignment allows more than one trait per character.

### Atticus Finch
- Teaches through reasoning, not authority: Ch3 porch compromise (keep
  reading secretly); Ch5 out-argues Jem into admitting the game exposed
  Boo's life. Technique: dialogue + actions. Real thematic weight, his
  defining parenting/moral philosophy.
- States the book's core lesson outright: "climb into his skin and walk
  around in it" (Ch3). Technique: dialogue, direct. Real thematic weight,
  arguably the thesis statement of the whole novel.

### Scout Finch
- Tomboyish, meets conflict physically: "I beat him up twice but it did
  no good" re: Dill (Ch5). Technique: narration (summary). Mostly color.
- Retrospective narrator who notices more than she says in the moment:
  "There was more to it than he knew, but I decided not to tell him"
  (Ch4); "Jem and I first began to part company" (Ch6). Technique:
  narration. Real thematic weight, flags a turning point for later.

### Jem Finch
- Fear of the Radley place is genuine under the bravado: hesitates at
  the gate after taunting Scout (Ch4); freezes/goes rigid at the shadow
  (Ch6). Technique: action-on-page. Real thematic weight, builds the
  suspense arc, strong book-vs-film material since it's visual.
- Values Atticus's trust over avoiding punishment: "Atticus ain't ever
  whipped me... I wanta keep it that way" (Ch6). Technique: dialogue,
  direct. Real thematic weight, a maturity beat.
- Thinks like a future lawyer, technicalities over spirit of the rule:
  renames characters to dodge a rule (Ch5); Atticus's "You want to be a
  lawyer, don't you?" Technique: actions + dialogue. Mostly color for
  now, worth revisiting once any courtroom material starts.

### Dill
- Compulsive embellisher/storyteller: "I helped the engineer" (Ch4);
  improvises the strip-poker excuse on the spot (Ch6). Technique:
  dialogue. Mostly color.

### Boo Radley (rumor-driven so far, no direct appearance)
- Town's fear culture blames him independent of facts: azaleas dying
  "because he breathed on them" (Ch1); suspicion outliving the Crazy
  Addie explanation (Ch1). Technique: narration/reported gossip
  (Others' Views). Real thematic weight, core to the rumor-vs-truth
  theme, characterizes the town as much as Boo.
- First firsthand account reframes him with sympathy: Miss Maudie "I
  remember Arthur Radley when he was a boy. He always spoke nicely to
  me... that is a sad house." (Ch5). Technique: dialogue (Others' Views,
  firsthand this time). Real thematic weight, a real turning point in
  how the reader's meant to see him.
