### Dill
- Ch 5 - "He had asked me earlier in the summer to marry him, then he promptly forgot about it... I beat him up twice but it did no good, he only grew closer to Jem."
  STEAL category: Actions. Technique: narration (summarizing a pattern, not one scene). Direct/indirect: indirect.
  Sets up Dill as fickle and drama-prone even with people he claims to love, and Scout's response (fighting, not sulking) reinforces her own tomboy characterization at the same time.

- Ch 5 - "I figure if he'd come out and sit a spell with us he might feel better."
  STEAL category: Speech. Technique: dialogue. Direct/indirect: indirect.
  Right after Jem lays out the mechanics of the note plan, Dill's actual motive turns out to be naive kindness, not thrill-seeking, which makes the scheme land as sweet and reckless at the same time.

### Jem
- Ch 5 - "he would simply change the names of the characters and then we couldn't be accused of playing anything"
  STEAL category: Thoughts/Actions. Technique: narration. Direct/indirect: indirect.
  Right after establishing Jem thinks Atticus's silence means permission, this loophole-hunting is Jem thinking like a future lawyer before anyone's called him one, technicality over spirit of the rule.

### Miss Maudie
- Ch 5 - swoops on a 3-inch blade of nut grass "like the Second Battle of the Marne," calls it a coming "Old Testament pestilence"
  STEAL category: Actions + Speech. Technique: action-on-page + dialogue (comic exaggeration). Direct/indirect: indirect.
  Her one exception to loving everything that grows shows real intensity and a dry, theatrical sense of humor underneath the gentle gardener image.

- Ch 5 - "I know he's alive, Jean Louise, because I haven't seen him carried out yet."
  STEAL category: Speech. Technique: dialogue. Direct/indirect: indirect.
  Answers Scout's morbid question with blunt, matter-of-fact logic instead of getting spooked or shutting the conversation down, showing she treats Scout like someone who can handle a real answer.

- Ch 5 - "That is three-fourths colored folks and one-fourth Stephanie Crawford," then the "move over and make room for him" line that "shut her up a while"
  STEAL category: Speech. Technique: dialogue. Direct/indirect: indirect.
  Shredding a rumor with a single sarcastic comeback shows her sharp tongue and her refusal to take gossip at face value, exactly the trait that makes Scout trust her over someone like Stephanie.

- Ch 5 - "I remember Arthur Radley when he was a boy. He always spoke nicely to me... that is a sad house."
  STEAL category: Speech + Thoughts. Technique: dialogue. Direct/indirect: mixed (the memory is indirect evidence about Arthur, but "that is a sad house" is Miss Maudie stating her judgment directly).
  This is the first time anyone in the book describes Boo/Arthur from actual firsthand memory instead of rumor, and her calling the house "sad" rather than scary reframes him as someone to pity, not fear.

### Atticus
- Ch 5 - "What Mr. Radley did was his own business... How would we like it if Atticus barged in on us without knocking, when we were in our rooms at night?"
  STEAL category: Speech. Technique: dialogue. Direct/indirect: direct (states his reasoning outright rather than leaving it to be inferred).
  Consistent with his Chapter 1/4 stance of respecting the Radleys' privacy, but here he explains the actual principle behind it instead of just warning the kids off.

- Ch 5 - traps Jem into admitting the game was "putting his life's history on display for the edification of the neighborhood," then asks "You want to be a lawyer, don't you?"
  STEAL category: Speech. Technique: dialogue. Direct/indirect: indirect.
  Instead of just punishing Jem, he out-argues him using Jem's own words, showing Atticus's characteristic move of teaching through reasoning rather than authority, and quietly needling Jem's pride at the same time.
